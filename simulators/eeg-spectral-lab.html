Why this comparison matters

EEG is still a core tool in routine diagnosis because it is cheap and shows epileptic discharges directly. The clinician reads the frequency content of the signal, so a cleaner spectrum means a more confident decision. The classic FFT-based estimate needs long frames and shows side-lobe peaks caused by windowing. AR modelling assumes a model of the signal, so it gives a smooth spectrum with sharp peaks even from few samples.

1. The periodogram (DFT/FFT based)

Take a frame of N samples, multiply it by a window w[n], compute the DFT and square its magnitude. Zero-padding only smooths the shape of the curve; the true resolution stays close to fs/N.

X[m] = Σ_{n=0}^{N-1} w[n]·x[n]·e^{-j2πmn/N} P_per(f_m) = |X[m]|² / (fs · Σ w²[n]) Δf ≈ fs / N

At fs = 50 Hz and N = 64 the resolution is about 0.78 Hz. The rectangular window has side lobes at −13 dB; Hanning pushes them down to −31 dB at the cost of a wider main lobe.

2. AR modelling (parametric)

Each sample is a linear combination of the previous p samples plus white noise e[n]. The signal is therefore the output of an all-pole IIR filter driven by white noise, which ties the method directly to the Z-transform chapter.

x[n] = − Σ_{m=1}^{p} a_m · x[n−m] + e[n] H(z) = 1 / (1 + a_1 z^{-1} + … + a_p z^{-p}) P_AR(f) = σ_p² Δt / | 1 + Σ_{m=1}^{p} a_m e^{−j2πfmΔt} |²

Each pole close to the unit circle makes a sharp peak; the pole angle sets its frequency.

3. Estimating the coefficients

Levinson-Durbin solves the Yule-Walker equations recursively from the autocorrelation R_xx(k):

a_11 = −R(1)/R(0) a_kk = −[ R(k) + Σ_{m=1}^{k-1} a_{k-1,m} R(k−m) ] / σ²_{k-1} a_ki = a_{k-1,i} + a_kk · a_{k-1,k-i} σ²_k = (1 − a_kk²) · σ²_{k-1}

Burg minimises the forward and backward prediction errors directly on the samples. It resolves better on short frames and always gives a stable filter.

4. Choosing the model order p

Too low an order hides the peaks; too high an order creates spurious ones. Akaike's criterion balances fit against complexity:

AIC(p) = N · ln(σ_p²) + 2p

The paper chose p between 10 and 15. In our runs at N = 64, AIC picked p = 12 more often than any other order.
